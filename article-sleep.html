You train hard, you eat clean... yet your results stall? What if the missing link was neither your program nor your diet, but your nights? Sleep is the most powerful (and most neglected) performance tool.

Why sleep determines your results

  • Growth hormone: about 70% is released during deep sleep — that's when muscles repair and grow.
  • Cortisol: lack of sleep raises this stress hormone, which promotes fat storage and muscle breakdown.
  • Energy and glycogen: short nights shrink the energy reserves for tomorrow's workout.
  • Focus and injury risk: slower reactions and poor coordination = higher injury risk.

How many hours do you really need?

The general recommendation is 7 to 9 hours per night. If you train regularly, aim for 8 to 10 hours: the higher your training load, the more recovery you need. Signs you're not sleeping enough: hard mornings, irritability, sugar cravings, declining performance.

7 habits for truly restorative sleep

  1. Go to bed and wake up at the same time, even on weekends.
  2. No screens for 1 hour before bed (blue light blocks melatonin).
  3. Keep your bedroom cool (65–67°F / 18–19°C), dark and quiet.
  4. No caffeine after 2 PM (its half-life lasts 6–8 hours).
  5. Light dinner, at least 2–3 hours before bedtime.
  6. Build a relaxing routine: reading, gentle stretching, breathing exercises.
  7. If falling asleep is still hard, a natural support (melatonin, herbs) can help short-term — see our recommendation below.
