Ashwagandha KSM-66 vs Sensoril: Which Extract Actually Works? (2026)

Published: October 12, 2026 · 10 min read · By FormeVital Team

Disclosure: This article contains affiliate links. We may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Ashwagandha is the most studied adaptogen in the world — over 200 clinical trials. But not all ashwagandha is created equal. The extract type, withanolide concentration, and extraction method make the difference between a supplement that transforms your stress response and one that does absolutely nothing.

The two dominant patented extracts are KSM-66 and Sensoril. They look similar on the label but work very differently. We dug into the clinical data and give you our honest verdict.

What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an adaptogenic herb used for over 3,000 years in Ayurvedic medicine. Modern research confirms it modulates cortisol, supports GABA pathways, improves thyroid function and enhances physical performance.

The active compounds are withanolides — steroidal lactones that interact with the HPA axis. Higher withanolide concentration = stronger clinical effects. This is where KSM-66 and Sensoril diverge dramatically.

KSM-66: The Full-Spectrum Root Extract

KSM-66 Ashwagandha

Manufacturer: Ixoreal Biomed (India) · Source: Root only · Withanolides: ≥5%

Clinical trials: 24+ published RCTs

  • Most clinically studied ashwagandha extract in the world
  • Root-only extraction (traditional Ayurvedic method)
  • Non-sedating — can be taken morning or evening
  • Improves testosterone (+15-17%), muscle strength and VO2 max
  • Reduces cortisol by 23–30% in clinical trials
  • Lower withanolide % than Sensoril (5% vs 10%)
  • Effects are more gradual (2–4 weeks for full benefit)

Sensoril: The High-Potency Leaf + Root Extract

Sensoril Ashwagandha

Manufacturer: Natreon Inc. (USA/India) · Source: Leaves + roots · Withanolides: ≥10%

Clinical trials: 8+ published studies

  • Double the withanolide concentration (10% vs 5%)
  • More sedating — better for evening use and sleep support
  • Faster onset of calming effects (some report same-day)
  • Lower effective dose needed (125–250mg vs 300–600mg)
  • Includes leaf extract (less traditional)
  • Fewer clinical trials than KSM-66
  • Can cause drowsiness — not ideal for daytime use

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ashwagandha safe to take every day?

Yes. Clinical trials show daily use for 8–12 weeks is safe with no serious adverse events. If you experience digestive upset, reduce the dose or take with food.

How long does it take for ashwagandha to work?

KSM-66: noticeable stress reduction in 2–4 weeks, full benefits at 8 weeks. Sensoril: calming effects within hours to days, full sleep benefits at 2–4 weeks.

Can I take ashwagandha with magnesium glycinate?

Yes — excellent combination. Magnesium glycinate calms the nervous system while ashwagandha modulates cortisol. Take magnesium glycinate (200–400mg) in the evening with Sensoril for maximum sleep support.

Does ashwagandha affect thyroid function?

Yes — ashwagandha can increase T3 and T4 levels. Beneficial for subclinical hypothyroidism but potentially problematic for hyperthyroidism. Consult your doctor if you have a thyroid condition.
