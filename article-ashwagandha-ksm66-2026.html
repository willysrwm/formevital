Ashwagandha is the most studied adaptogen in the world — over 200 clinical trials, thousands of participants, decades of Ayurvedic use. But here's the problem: not all ashwagandha is created equal. The extract type, withanolide concentration, and extraction method make the difference between a supplement that transforms your stress response and one that does absolutely nothing.

The two dominant patented extracts on the market are KSM-66 and Sensoril. They look similar on the label but work very differently in the body. We dug into the clinical data, compared withanolide profiles, and give you our honest verdict on which one to choose — and when.

What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an adaptogenic herb used for over 3,000 years in Ayurvedic medicine. Modern research confirms it modulates cortisol, supports GABA pathways, improves thyroid function and enhances physical performance.

The active compounds are withanolides — steroidal lactones that interact with the HPA axis (your stress response system). Higher withanolide concentration = stronger clinical effects. This is where KSM-66 and Sensoril diverge dramatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ashwagandha safe to take every day?

Yes. Clinical trials show daily use for 8–12 weeks is safe with no serious adverse events. Some practitioners recommend cycling (5 days on, 2 days off) but there's no clinical evidence this is necessary. If you experience digestive upset, reduce the dose or take with food.

How long does it take for ashwagandha to work?

KSM-66: noticeable stress reduction in 2–4 weeks, full benefits at 8 weeks. Sensoril: calming effects can be felt within hours to days, full sleep benefits at 2–4 weeks. Consistency is key — don't judge effectiveness before 4 weeks of daily use.

Can I take ashwagandha with magnesium glycinate?

Yes — this is an excellent combination. Magnesium glycinate calms the nervous system while ashwagandha modulates cortisol. Take magnesium glycinate (200–400mg) in the evening with Sensoril for maximum sleep support, or with KSM-66 in the morning for stress resilience.

Does ashwagandha affect thyroid function?

Yes — ashwagandha can increase T3 and T4 levels. This is beneficial for people with subclinical hypothyroidism but potentially problematic for those with hyperthyroidism. If you have a thyroid condition, consult your doctor before starting ashwagandha.

KSM-66 vs generic ashwagandha: is the premium worth it?

Absolutely. Generic ashwagandha powder has variable withanolide content (often 0.5–2%), meaning you'd need 10–20x the dose to match KSM-66's effects. Patented extracts guarantee standardization, clinical backing and purity. The price difference is small ($0.30–0.50/day) compared to the efficacy gap.
